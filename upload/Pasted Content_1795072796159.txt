3. ENGAGEMENT ARCHITECTURE
================================================================
Every system must feed one of three loops.

LOOP 1 — 2 MINUTES: "one more fight"
- Style Rank (D to S) scores move variety, no damage taken, environment use and clean counters.
- S-rank pays down debt. The player chooses between a fast clean win and a flashy one.
- Instant arena retry (10 seconds) for optional arena and contract fights. Story fights stay Death = Debt.
- Every core job finishable in about 5 minutes, with checkpoints at Beat boundaries.
- Replay clip: the last 10 seconds of the best combo auto-saves.

LOOP 2 — 30 MINUTES: the Ledger puzzle
Each day has 3 Beats (Dusk, Midnight, 3 AM). Every call, job or hangout spends one Beat. The Ledger screen shows: debts due (rate, collector), pending calls, friend trust and days since contact, and Heat per faction and per friend. The player decides who to pay, who to ignore, who to fight, and which Beat to spend where. Paying one faction early raises another's interest. Skipping a friend lowers trust. Nothing is free.

LOOP 3 — 20+ HOURS: story and replay
- Unscripted kidnap target (Trust rules below).
- Faction paths change available styles, tools and endings.
- New Game+ with Carried Debt: the city remembers your last life.
- Hidden calls, texts that change meaning on a second playthrough, secret scenes with the Drunk.

ETHICAL ENGAGEMENT (hard rules)
- No energy timers, no pay-to-win, no loot boxes, no daily login rewards, no streak guilt.
- Leaderboards (if any) are friends-only and optional.
- Engagement comes from discovery, mastery, meaningful decisions, unpredictable situations, personal investment and a world that responds intelligently. Not grinding.

4. DEEP GAMEPLAY SYSTEMS
================================================================

4A. COMBAT
Five martial styles, learned from people, never lost:
- Brawler (Brother): balanced, armored shoulder-barge counter, brutal environmental slams.
- Kickboxing (Bouncer): fast, low-kick knee-buckles, slip/duck liver-blow counters, breaks guards.
- Grappling (Collector): catch-parries, human-shield grabs, bowling-ball throws into crowds.
- Drunken (The Drunk): unpredictable cadence, automatic high-attack stumble-evades, crowd control.
- Weapon (Lieutenant): bat, pipe, knife; high lethality, fragile durability.

Rules:
1. Style countering is a SOFT bonus, not a lock. The right style gives a generous parry window and extra posture damage. The wrong style is weaker, never helpless.
2. Attack tokens: at most 2 to 3 enemies attack at once. The rest circle, taunt or flank.
3. Posture break: blocks and parries fill the enemy's posture bar. A full bar opens a finisher. The player has Composure on the same model: wild swinging breaks your own guard.
4. Adaptive AI: enemies track last_3_player_moves and counter repeated strings. Spamming one style makes enemy groups spawn wearing its counter.
5. Enemy personalities with recognisable attack tells and punish windows. Group coordination, flanking and spacing.
6. Dirty Street Tactics: one Dirty Trick per style (ash/water blind, foot-stomp pin, jacket-over-head blind). Grab snaps to walls, neon signs, car doors and noodle pots within 2 m. Each location has 2 to 3 unique environmental finishers.
7. Defensive play must be as rewarding as aggressive play.
8. Lingering injuries: cracked ribs or bruised hands persist, slowing stamina regen or dodge until treated at the Street Doc (clean cash or a favor) or numbed with vision-blurring painkillers.
9. Adaptive difficulty through debt: after repeated losses, interest softens or a collector offers a grace window. Enemies never quietly get weaker.
10. The player should feel more capable through skill and learned techniques, not bigger damage numbers.

Combat feel reference (tune in the inspector):
- Light attack: hitstop 3 to 4 frames, small directional shake, short rumble/haptic.
- Heavy attack: hitstop 8 to 10 frames, stronger shake, longer haptic, stronger knockback, optional launch, slight FOV kick.
- Cancel window: light attacks can cancel into dodge between about 150 and 350 ms. Heavy attacks cannot cancel.
- Eased hitstop recovery over about 4 frames.
- Parry: generous window with the right style, heavy hitstop, distinct high sound and haptic.
- Input buffering, animation blending (upper body attacks while lower body moves) and input leniency everywhere.

4B. BORROWING POWER (debt changes the fight)
The player can pull a Corporation firearm or an experimental shock-gauntlet mid-fight. Every bullet or tech burst ticks a live Debt/Favor counter on screen. Each lender attaches a Perk and a String:

| Lender | Perk | String |
|---|---|---|
| The Family | Backup arrives mid-fight | No guns in their sight; they take a cut of every win |
| The Corporation | Pistol, shock-gauntlet, tech tools | Remote kill-switch: missed payments lock the item mid-firefight |
| The Crew | Free help, no interest | Crew members get hurt if you lose; they need you |

Interest ticks in real time. The longer a debt is ignored, the more collectors appear INSIDE your fights, not on a menu.
Scarred style: heavy debt to a faction adds their moves to your set and removes some of yours.
(Deferred tier ladder: weapons, guns, tech, mech. Guns and the shock-gauntlet come first; mech and beyond are "if funded".)

4C. THE PHONE AS GAMEPLAY
- Calls interrupt fights. The low buzz hits mid-brawl and the player has a few seconds to answer while enemies keep swinging.
- Demand (faction) versus Invitation (friend). One answer per ring. Declining a demand costs safety. Declining an invitation costs trust.
- Leverage Clips: factions send a photo or clip of something you did. Declining means it gets posted and crowds react.
- Texts with countdowns, replayable voicemails with clues, voicemail from Estranged friends.
- Last Call: once per act, every faction and friend calls at once. You can answer only one.
- The phone buzz is a low, slow pulse: the tension sound of the game. When the phone UI opens, the world dims and slows.

4D. TRUST RULES (tunable)
Each friend has Trust 0 to 100.
- Starts at 70. Loses 6 per Beat of unanswered calls. +15 for a hangout. +10 for a clean setup. -10 if a setup gets them hurt.
- Close: 60 and above. Drifting: 30 to 59. Estranged: below 30.
- Close friends help in a fight once. Drifting friends hesitate. Estranged friends stop calling and will not help.
- The Act 3 kidnap target is the friend with the LOWEST Trust when the trigger fires. Ties go to the friend with the highest Heat.
- The Ledger always shows Trust, so the outcome is earned and visible.

Friends: Brother (Brawler, family, default kidnap target if neglected), Mechanic (garage, repairs, power-cut setup), Club Owner (laundering, job info, brawl-staging setup), The Drunk (Drunken style, best dialogue, lures cops). Each has ONE hangout, all using the same timing-minigame skeleton, reskinned: Fishing, Basketball, Darts, Karaoke. Hangouts are instanced scenes, no companion AI.

4E. THE WEB OF LEVERAGE (investigation and extortion board)
- Reached from the apartment wall or an encrypted phone app.
- Players link clues from interrogating stunned enemies, tailing targets, cloning phones, planting bugs or buying rumors from the bartender.
- Connecting two pieces of dirt unlocks Extortion Dialogue: cancel debts, force safe passage, turn factions against each other.
- Clues can go stale or be countered.

4F. SANDBOX MISSIONS (Recon, Setup, Execution)
1. Recon: eavesdrop for keypad codes, bribe staff, scout exits.
2. Friend Tactical Setups: Mechanic cuts block power for 45 s; Club Owner stages a dance-floor brawl to pull guards; Drunk lures cops away. Using a friend raises their Heat. Overuse gets their business hit.
3. Clean Hands versus Wrecking Ball: every mission is beatable with zero kills (stealth or blackmail) or through loud combat with heavy fallout.
Job templates (reskinned): Delivery, Rescue, Coerced, Hit, Turf. Ten core jobs total. Missions can change through NPC decisions, faction interference, recon evidence, betrayals, reputation, previous outcomes, and time and location.

4G. DOUBLE-AGENT ECONOMY
- Dirty Cash (faction hits) versus Clean Cash (legitimate shops, clinic).
- Launder through the Club Owner (20% cut) or gamble in back-room dice and card games with sleight-of-hand cheating.
- DEATH = DEBT: losing never reloads a save. You wake in a gutter or clinic owing whoever patched you up. Defaulting triggers a city-wide manhunt.

4H. THE KIDNAP ARC
Four missions: (1) Cold Call (coerced), (2) Investigation (NO combat, a pace change), (3) Assault (arena, eight archetypes in waves, one mini-boss), (4) Choice. Save them: lose the money, the faction becomes a permanent enemy, the friend is Close forever. Take the payoff: lose the friend, the faction becomes a patron, the city hollows out. Both endings are real.

4I. THE REACTIVE CITY
- Persistent decals, scorch marks, boarded windows, memorials, thinning crowds.
- Rumors: street NPCs gossip about what you actually did, with details that grow or distort over Beats.
- Prices and doors: shops raise prices, close early or refuse you. A radio recaps your week.
- Heat tiers with named responses: fixers first, then hitmen who fight in your own style.
- Crowds are witnesses: fights on crowded streets scatter witnesses, someone films it, faction heat rises. Fights in alleys have no witnesses. Crowds impede both player and enemies.

5. DEEP ENGAGEMENT, PLAYER FREEDOM AND EMERGENT GAMEPLAY
================================================================
CORE: Engagement must come from discovery, mastery, meaningful decisions, unpredictable situations, personal investment and a world that responds intelligently. The player should regularly think: "What happens if I try this?", "Who is behind this?", "What happens if I don't intervene?"

A. A city that generates its own stories
- NPCs keep schedules, relationships, rivalries, goals and memories. Factions compete for territory, money, influence and information. Businesses prosper, struggle, close or become faction-controlled. Witnesses remember violence and spread rumors. Characters act without waiting for the player.
- Examples: a shopkeeper you protected tips you off to a robbery; a gang takes over a street you use; a defeated opponent returns with allies and has studied your habits; a friend makes a deal behind your back because your choices put their business at risk.
- Events must emerge from understandable rules, not arbitrary randomness.

B. Player-defined reputation
No fixed hero or villain identity. Identities emerge from behavior: underground fighting champion, neighbourhood protector, criminal strategist, professional fixer, business operator, information broker, faction manipulator, unpredictable independent. Reputation affects dialogue, opportunities, prices, intimidation, recruitment, faction reactions and NPC trust.

C. Dynamic sandbox missions
Example: contract to recover a stolen package. On arrival it has already been sold, the buyer is negotiating with another faction, and your contact is secretly involved. Solutions: negotiation, theft, blackmail, deception, intimidation or combat. Outcomes affect future missions, relationships, faction power and opportunities.

D. Exploration and discovery
Hidden techniques and training, underground tournaments, secret businesses and illegal markets, abandoned locations with evidence, hidden routes, personal stories of persistent NPCs, rival hideouts, unusual encounters. Not every discovery gives money or gear; some give knowledge, relationships or permanent changes to your understanding of the city.

E. Long-term goals (connected, no XP grind)
Master all five styles; build financial independence; establish legitimate businesses; repair friendships; manipulate faction relationships; uncover the district's history; acquire rare gear and techniques; influence territory; complete personal challenges; change the district's future.

F. THE EMERGENT RULE
One player action should create several connected consequences. Example: you win an underground tournament -> a recording spreads -> a promoter offers a contract -> a rival challenges you -> a faction offers to rig the next tournament -> a friend warns the promoter is exploiting you.

G. Implementation: no city simulation. Use ONE shared world-state table (named variables), an EVENT QUEUE, data-driven NPC schedules (Resources), reusable AI behaviors and mission templates. Simulate a small district deeply enough to feel alive.

THE LEVERAGE DESIGN STANDARD
Combine immediate, short-term, long-term and emergent engagement. The player must never feel the only thing to do is follow the next marker. Every major feature must answer: What does the player do? What makes it interesting? What decisions exist? How does the system respond? What changes afterward? Why replay it? Avoid features that sound impressive but play shallow.

8. GODOT 4 TECHNICAL AND VISUAL STANDARDS
================================================================
RENDERING AND PERFORMANCE
- Renderer: MOBILE. Never Forward+ for phone exports.
- Frame budget: 60 fps on iPhone 16, locked 30 fps on iPhone 13 mini. Physics at 30 ticks/second. Lower crowd cap on the floor device. Expose a quality setting.
- Textures VRAM compressed (ETC2 and ASTC enabled).
- Baked occlusion for the blocks (Mobile has no depth prepass). LODs for NPCs, imposters for distant buildings.
- Lights: one baked or emissive neon look, at most 2 to 3 dynamic lights near the player. Blob shadows for NPCs. Lower shadow quality first when frames dip.
- Shader pre-warming on scene load to avoid first-use stutter.
- CPU particles for blood and sparks (work on every renderer).
- Include an on-screen FPS and draw-call debug toggle so I can report numbers from my phone.

VISUAL STYLE
- High-contrast neon noir: deep blue-black shadows, wet asphalt, emissive Magenta #FF007F and Cyan #00F0FF accents. Always night, always rain.
- Low-poly characters, Fresnel rim lighting, inverted-hull outlines for a comic-noir read.
- One post-process pass only: contrast, bloom, light chromatic aberration.
- Impact frames: a one-frame white or inverted flash and brief speed lines on heavy hits. Finishers get a letterbox and slow-mo camera.
- Weather: camera-anchored vertex-shader rain with indoor masking, screen-space droplets, ground ripple decals. No heavy particle rain.
- Wet asphalt: low roughness plus scrolling ripple normal maps. No SSR.
- Height fog so distant blocks dissolve into pink and cyan haze.

COMBAT ARCHITECTURE
- Custom AttackData Resource: damage, hitstop_frames, knockback_vector, shake_intensity, posture_damage, cancel_start_ms, cancel_end_ms, haptic_pattern, audio_layers.
- SELECTIVE hitstop: pause attacker and target AnimationTree and velocity for 3 to 8 frames. NEVER use global Engine.time_scale for hitstop. Rain, particles and audio keep running.
- Animation-driven hitboxes (AnimationPlayer method tracks) on Area3D. Hurtbox on layer 20 with monitorable = true; Hitbox checks get_overlapping_areas() on its activation frame.
- 8-directional stagger reactions (start with 4, expand later). Directional camera shake. Eased hitstop recovery.
- Ring-buffer pooling for persistent blood decals (cap 150).
- Reuse state machines, data Resources and mission templates.

AUDIO
- Six-layer impact: whoosh, cloth, impact, sub-bass bone thud, voice grunt, room tail. Build 3 layers first (impact, bone, voice) then add the rest. Use generated placeholder sounds if no files exist.
- Route through AudioEffectCompressor plus Limiter. Duck rain and music by -4 dB on heavy hits.
- Low drone under everything that rises in pitch near danger. The phone buzz is the tension sound.
